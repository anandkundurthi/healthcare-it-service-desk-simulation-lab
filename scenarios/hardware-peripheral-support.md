# Scenario: Windows Hardware and Peripheral Support

## Scenario
A fictional clinic employee reports that their workstation cannot detect an external keyboard. The built-in keyboard works. No patient information is involved.

## Intake questions
- When did the issue start?
- Is the keyboard connected by USB or another supported connection?
- Does the keyboard show any indicator light?
- Does another approved USB port work?
- Does the issue affect only this workstation?
- Has the device or cable been physically damaged?

## Safe first-level checks
1. Confirm the issue and scope with the user.
2. Check that the cable/receiver is firmly connected.
3. If permitted, test another approved port.
4. Check whether Windows displays a device notification.
5. Open Device Manager and look for a device warning; record the exact displayed status.
6. Do not uninstall drivers, change BIOS settings, or open equipment unless authorized.

## Record
- Device type and fictional asset label
- Symptoms and scope
- Checks performed and observed results
- Whether the issue was resolved
- Next action or escalation reason

## Escalate when
- Physical damage or electrical risk is suspected.
- The device remains unavailable after approved basic checks.
- Administrator access or driver deployment is required.
- Multiple workstations are affected.

## Practice outcome
Do not assume the keyboard is fixed. Record the actual outcome of your rehearsal.
